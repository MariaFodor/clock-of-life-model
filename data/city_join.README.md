# `city_join.csv` — the key mapping between two city datasets

Generated by `experiments/exp16_city_join.py` (W-B0). Regenerate with:

```
PYTHONPATH=src .venv/bin/python experiments/exp16_city_join.py
```

## What it is, and what it deliberately is not

A **key mapping only**. Each row says "this greenspace city is this WHO settlement, and here is how
far apart their centroids are", or says why it is not. It carries **no measured value** — no PM2.5,
no NDVI — and a test should keep it that way.

That shape is a licence decision, not a stylistic one. WHO's Ambient Air Quality Database is
**CC BY-NC-SA 3.0 IGO**, whose ShareAlike term attaches to adaptations; Stowell's greenspace data is
**CC0 1.0**. Keeping the two sets of measurements in their own files and joining by key means the
ShareAlike obligation attaches to WHO-derived files — including this one, which carries WHO's
settlement names and a distance derived from WHO's coordinates — rather than to everything
downstream of a merged table.

**This file is WHO-derived and inherits CC BY-NC-SA 3.0 IGO.**

## Method

The design's stated method was "match on GHS urban-centre id, or centroids within a stated tolerance,
never name similarity". That is impossible with the published files: Stowell's table carries a city
name and a country name and nothing else — no coordinates, no identifier — even though it was derived
from the GHS Urban Centre Database, whose ids were dropped at publication. WHO's side carries
coordinates for 100% of its PM2.5 settlements.

So the decisive hop is geographic and the first hop is a gazetteer lookup, because the data leaves no
alternative:

1. greenspace city name → GeoNames `cities15000` (including alternate names) → coordinates
2. coordinates → nearest WHO settlement **in the same country**, by great-circle distance
3. accept within the declared tolerance; otherwise write the rejection and its reason

Step 1 is what makes the join work at all: WHO records local endonyms, so *Bucharest → Bucureşti*
(1.0 km), *Warsaw → Warszawa* (0.8 km), *Milan → Milano* (2.0 km). An exact-name join finds 383 of
these; going through the gazetteer finds **527**.

## Counts, at the declared 25 km tolerance

| | |
|---|---|
| greenspace cities | 1,038 |
| **joined to a measured PM2.5 settlement** | **527** |
| rejected — nearest measured settlement too far | 351 |
| rejected — country has no PM2.5 measurement at all | 101 |
| rejected — city not in the gazetteer | 59 |

Sensitivity: **464** within 10 km, **527** within 25 km, **599** within 50 km.

For context, air alone covers **5,311 settlements in 116 countries** (WHO publishes 8,023 settlements,
but 2,712 of them carry only PM10 or NO₂, not the PM2.5 the model prices).
